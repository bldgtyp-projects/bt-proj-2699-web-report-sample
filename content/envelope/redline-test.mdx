dek: "Trace one continuous air barrier around the conditioned envelope and flag the critical junctions."

Illustrated below is an example of the "red line" evaluation test. This test, commonly used in Passive House design,
requires that a single continuous air barrier (commonly denoted with a red line) can be traced around the entire
conditioned envelope of the home. Any area where the air barrier line is broken or ambiguous is marked for further
development and clarification by the design team.

The goal of this exercise is to identify and clearly document all critical junctions, transitions, and penetrations of
the air barrier. In the diagram below, the areas marked with callouts are judged to be critical details, and we
recommend that the detailing phase address these items and clearly identify the airtightness layers, products, and
transition details. Establishing an unambiguous air barrier with all needed project details will be critical to
achieving the high-performance targets.

1. **Typical floor slab:** Use the sub-slab vapor barrier as the airtight layer. Tape / seal all joints, penetrations,
   and seams. Sub-slab vapor barrier to be minimum 15 mil sheeting, installed above the insulation in direct contact
   with the concrete.
2. **Detail, slab to wall:** Turn the sub-slab vapor barrier up the wall a minimum of 12" and seal with elastic sealant
   and tape. Use a termination bar to ensure a permanent bond.
3. **Typical wall:** The airtightness layer is the EXTERIOR sheathing layer. Seal all joints, seams, and penetrations
   per the manufacturer's instructions.
4. **Typical roof:** The airtightness layer is the INTERIOR air barrier / vapor-control membrane. Install the membrane
   before framing interior partitions to ensure continuity. Seal all joints, seams, and penetrations per the
   manufacturer's instructions. Recommended: install a service cavity (drop ceiling) to avoid penetrations through this
   layer.
5. **Detail, window connections:** Connect windows and doors to the exterior airtight layer.
