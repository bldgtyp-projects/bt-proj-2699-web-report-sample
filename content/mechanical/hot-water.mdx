#### Split heat-pump water heater

For hot water generation we strongly recommend a high-performance electric heat pump. For this project we recommend the
SanCO2 split heat pump to provide all hot water for the home. The heat pump is located on the exterior of the building,
moving heat from the outdoors to the water tank. This leads to very high efficiency and low energy consumption.
Importantly, it uses CO<sub>2</sub> as its refrigerant, which has a much lower global-warming impact than typical cooling
refrigerants. Globally, refrigerant leakage is one of the primary drivers of near-term warming, and many groups have
identified refrigerant management and low-impact refrigerants as a key part of any strategy to reduce climate change.

For more information refer to [Small Planet Supply](https://www.smallplanetsupply.com/sanc02).

#### All-in-one heat-pump water heater

Another option is an all-in-one air-source heat-pump water heater such as those offered by
[Rheem](https://www.rheem.com/products/residential/water-heating/heat-pump-water-heaters/),
[State](https://www.statewaterheaters.com/products/residential/electric/proline/xe/heat-pump/), and others. These units
mount the heat pump directly on the tank, making them easier to install and maintain than split configurations. They are
powered by electricity, making them cleaner and healthier than oil or gas-fired options, and have a very high level of
energy efficiency.

The downside of all-in-one units compared to split units is that they are noisier, they need a relatively large indoor
area to pull heat from, and they cool the space around them. In most cases they cannot be installed in a tight enclosed
closet without suffering performance issues. A unit like this could be installed in a mechanical room using the optional
ducted installation, though we prefer the split heat pump for noise, comfort, and overall efficiency reasons.

#### Shower drain-water heat recovery

To reduce hot-water energy consumption and extend the capacity of the hot-water tank, we recommend installing an in-line
shower drain-water heat recovery element. This simple element has no moving parts and saves 15-25% of the energy in
drain water which would otherwise be lost to the waste-water system. Given the modest first cost and quick payback, we
strongly recommend a product of this sort. A vertical unit could be installed below the main bathroom shower; if that is
impractical, a horizontal unit could be installed in the basement. Some product options include:

- [RenewABILITY Power-Pipe](https://renewability.com/)
- [EcoInnovation ThermoDrain](https://ecoinnovation.ca/)
- [Ecodrain](https://ecodrain.com/en/)
