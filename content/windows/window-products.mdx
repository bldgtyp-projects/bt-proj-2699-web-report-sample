For this project, the Code-Minimum variant uses windows which just meet the <VarLink
hrefKey="narrative.energy_code.u_val_link" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><Var
k="narrative.energy_code.name" /> / <Var k="narrative.energy_code.zone" /> limit (U-<Var
k="narrative.energy_code.window_min_u_value" /> Btu/hr-ft2-F)</VarLink>.

As discussed above, the Passive House frameworks recommend windows with an installed U-value (Uw-installed) of less than
<Var k="narrative.windows.ph_window_u_value" /> Btu/hr-ft2-F in order to satisfy winter thermal comfort goals. To
achieve the challenging heating and cooling annual energy demand limits, this project should use windows with an
installed U-value of less than <Var k="narrative.windows.ph_window_u_value" /> Btu/hr-ft2-F, and follow the Passive
House recommendations where possible.

While this is a challenging requirement, there are now several cost-competitive high-performance window products
available in the North American market. Some good options which can provide windows with a U-value of <Var
k="narrative.windows.ph_window_u_value" /> Btu/hr-ft2-F or better include:

- [Ikon](https://www.ikonwindows.com/)
- [Zola](https://www.zolawindows.com/)
- [Optiwin](http://www.optiwin.net/en/)
- [Yaro](http://www.yarowindows.com/)
- [European Architectural Supply](https://www.eas-usa.com/)
- [Tanner](https://www.tannerwindows.com/)
